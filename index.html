How we decide the labels
  • Top pick (30 orgs): the most likely to return and the best fit for our students. Selected by Google every year from 2022 to 2026 and in at least 8 of the 11 years since 2016; took 5 or more students in 2026; works mainly in web, Python, Java or mobile; and is not rated Advanced.
  • Likely: in 2026 and in at least 3 of the 5 years from 2022 to 2026. Possible: new in 2026, or in 2025 but not 2026. Google announces the 2027 orgs around February, so these are forecasts from track record, not promises.
  • Beginner: the org’s main listed technology is Python, JavaScript/TypeScript, Java, Kotlin, Flutter, PHP or similar; it has mentored in 3 or more GSoC years; and it took 4 or more students in its latest year. Advanced: its main technology is C, C++, Rust, Go or similar, or it lists compilers, kernels, LLVM or hardware. Intermediate: everything else.
  • Code repo: taken from the org’s own links on its official GSoC page, or from the code links of its past GSoC projects, then checked that the page exists. “Contribution guide” is the guide the org lists on its official GSoC page.
  • Umbrella: one org that hosts many projects. Its “Project list” links to each sub-project and its repo. Pick one sub-project; difficulty varies.
  • Sources: official GSoC archive, compiled by the open gsoc-organizations dataset.

How to start

Aim to get 2 or 3 pull requests merged in one org before the 2027 ideas lists come out in February.

    Do and don’t

    Do

    • Pick a bug you can reproduce, and add a test with your fix
    • Ask in the project’s public chat or on the issue
    • Read the code first, then ask one clear question
    • Link every pull request in your proposal

    Don’t

    • Send private messages to maintainers
    • Write “please guide me” or “how do I start?”
    • Claim an issue that already has an open pull request
    • Spread yourself across five orgs

    A question that gets answers

    I’m working on #1234 (link). In module/foo.py, bar() is called from both X and Y, but only X resets the cache. Is that intentional, or should Y do the same? I traced it as far as baz() and wasn’t sure.
